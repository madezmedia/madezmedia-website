Part 01 · The 7 Evaluation Points

What your AI assistant must actually do.

Before you sign with any vendor, go through each of these 7 points. If they can't pass all 7, you're not buying an AI — you're renting a slightly smarter voicemail system.

  • 1

    Answer calls the way a knowledgeable staff member would

    The AI must handle real questions — not just "let me transfer you." It should answer FAQs about your services, pricing, hours, locations, and common objections without transferring. If it transfers everything, it's not an AI assistant. It's a routing system.

    Why it matters: The whole point is eliminating call volume. If it transfers 80% of calls, your staff is still answering the phone.
  • 2

    Capture leads even when it can't help

    For questions it can't answer, the AI must collect the caller's info, reason about why they called, and hand off in a structured format — not just "left a message." It should capture: what they wanted, urgency level, and the best time + method to call them back.

    Why it matters: Most callers hang up if they can't get answers. The AI should be better at capturing the lead than a missed call would be.
  • 3

    Integrate with your existing tools (CRM, calendar, messaging)

    The AI must write to your CRM, book appointments on your calendar, and send your team a notification — without you manually entering data after every call. If it just sends you a transcript, that's not integration.

    Why it matters: Manual follow-up is where leads die. If your staff has to enter call notes by hand, the AI is adding work, not removing it.
  • 4

    Handle multilingual and dialect variation

    In markets with significant non-English speaking populations, the AI must handle accented English and other languages naturally. "We don't support that yet" is not an acceptable answer in 2026.

    Why it matters: If 20% of your callers hang up because they can't communicate with the AI, you're losing 1 in 5 potential customers.
  • 5

    Sound like your business, not a robot

    The AI must adapt to your brand voice — not deliver everything in generic corporate-speak. It should be able to use your industry terminology correctly, match your tone (warm, professional, casual), and represent your business accurately.

    Why it matters: If a caller says "that sounds like an AI," you've already lost trust. The best AI assistants are indistinguishable from a well-trained receptionist.
  • 6

    Escalate intelligently — not just "can I speak with someone?"

    The AI must know when a situation needs human intervention (angry customer, medical emergency, legal question, high-value prospect) and escalate with full context — not just "transferring you." It should brief the human before the transfer happens.

    Why it matters: Blind transfers create the worst customer experience. A good AI briefs the human first, so the customer doesn't have to repeat everything.
  • 7

    Give you visibility into what's happening

    You need a dashboard or daily report that shows: call volume, call outcomes, peak hours, common questions, conversion rate, and failed handoffs. If you can't see it, you can't manage it.

    Why it matters: Running blind is worse than not running at all. You need to know if the AI is handling calls well or if it's creating a negative experience you're not seeing.
The one-question gut check: After a week of use, ask yourself: "Did my staff have to handle fewer calls this week?" If the answer isn't clearly yes — the AI is not doing its job. Keep evaluating.

Part 02 · The Hard Questions

Questions that expose the gaps.

Ask every AI phone vendor these. The ones who can't answer clearly are selling marketing, not AI.

  • What percentage of calls does your AI handle fully without human transfer? (Acceptable: 60%+)
  • What happens when the AI doesn't know the answer? How does it handle uncertainty?
  • Can it book appointments directly into our scheduling system without a human in the loop?
  • How long does it take to train on our specific business, services, and common questions?
  • What does a typical "failed handoff" look like? How do you notify us?
  • How does it handle heavy accents, background noise, and cross-talk?
  • Can we review and edit how it responds to specific questions after training?
  • What's your actual uptime SLA and what happens if the system goes down mid-call?
  • How do you measure success? What metrics do you track and share?
  • What does the onboarding process look like and who owns it?
Pro tip: Ask for a live demo with YOUR business's actual scenarios — not their canned demo. "Show me how it handles a caller asking about pricing for a service we don't advertise on our website." If they can't or won't, that's your answer.

Part 03 · Red Flags

5 signs the AI will disappoint you.

These are the warning signs that the vendor is selling hype, not a working system. Walk away or demand proof before signing.

  • "Our AI learns on its own." — Real AI assistants require training. "It learns on its own" means they haven't built the training infrastructure. Ask what the training process looks like.
  • Pricing is "starting at $99/mo" with no range. — Real AI phone systems cost $250-$2,000/month depending on call volume and features. Rock-bottom pricing means hidden fees or a system that handles very little.
  • No trial period or pilot option. — If they won't let you test it with real calls for 3-7 days, they're not confident in the product. A 30-day pilot is standard for real AI vendors.
  • They use generic voice cloning with no brand tuning. — If every business sounds the same, the AI wasn't built for your business. You should sound like you, not like a stock voice assistant.
  • No dashboard, no reporting, no call logs. — If you can't see what's happening, you can't improve it. Run. The vendor either doesn't know what's happening or doesn't want you to see it.
